Know your three papers

Three papers, one qualification. Each is 1 hour 30, worth 80 marks, and counts exactly a third. Any topic can appear on any paper — you cannot revise "by paper", only by topic.

Paper 1 · 8300/1

Non-calculator

1h 30m · 80 marks · 33⅓% · Fri 14 May 2027 (am)
  • No calculator — arithmetic is being tested on purpose
  • Fractions often beat decimals here: ⅓ is exact, 0.333… is not
  • Know your times tables, squares to 15², cubes of 1–5 and 10
  • Exact trig values (sin 30° = ½ etc.) love this paper
Golden rule: show working for every mark. A wrong answer with correct method still earns method marks — a bare wrong answer earns nothing.
Paper 2 · 8300/2

Calculator

1h 30m · 80 marks · 33⅓% · Thu 27 May 2027 (am)
  • Calculator allowed — and expected. Bring a scientific one you know
  • Write down the full calculator display before rounding
  • Use the fraction button, S⇔D, and ANS to avoid re-typing
  • Check DEGREE mode before any trigonometry
Examiner intel: "You must show your working" means it — a correct answer alone can score zero on those questions.
Paper 3 · 8300/3

Calculator

1h 30m · 80 marks · 33⅓% · Mon 14 Jun 2027 (am)
  • Same rules as Paper 2 — and the last exam of the series
  • Sat over two weeks after Paper 1: keep revising between papers
  • Topics that didn't appear on Papers 1–2 very often turn up here
  • After each paper, list what hasn't come up yet — then revise that
Strategy: the gap between papers is revision time most students waste. Papers 1 and 2 tell you exactly what Paper 3 is likely to ask.

Where the marks live — topic weightings

Set by Ofqual, identical for every exam board. This is your revision-time budget: Higher students, algebra is nearly a third of your exam. Foundation students, number and ratio are half of yours.

The three assessment objectives — what a question is really testing

AOWeighting F / HWhat it testsHow to spot it
AO150% / 40%Use and apply standard techniques — recall, notation, routine procedures."Work out", "Solve", "Simplify", "Factorise". Do the method, show the steps.
AO225% / 30%Reason, interpret and communicate mathematically."Show that", "Prove", "Give a reason", "Explain why". Every line must follow logically — and end with a conclusion.
AO325% / 30%Solve problems — translate a situation into maths, choose the method yourself.Wordy, real-life setups with no named method. Your first job is deciding what maths this is.

Command words — the question tells you the answer's shape

CommandIt means
Write down / StateNo working needed — it's a 1-mark recall or read-off. Don't waste time.
Work out / CalculateWorking IS needed. "Calculate" hints the calculator is useful (Papers 2–3).
EstimateRound every value to 1 significant figure first, then calculate. Do NOT work exactly and round at the end — that scores zero.
Show thatThe answer is given — the marks are ALL in the working. Reach the printed result with clear, complete steps.
ProveFormal reasoning: algebra set up in general terms (2n, 2n+1…), every step justified, and a closing statement.
HenceYou MUST use the previous part. "Hence or otherwise" — "hence" is nearly always faster.
Simplify fully / Factorise fully"Fully" is a warning: one more step exists. 2(2x+4) is not fully factorised; 4(x+2) is.
Give a reasonQuote the geometric fact by its full name: "alternate angles are equal", not "Z angles".
You must show your workingAn unsupported answer — even a correct one — can score zero on this question.

How your script is actually marked

AQA examiners award marks by type. Once you know the codes, "show your working" stops being teacher advice and becomes a scoring strategy.

MarkNameEarned by
M1Method markA correct method, even with wrong numbers carried in. This is why working = marks.
A1Accuracy markThe correct answer following a correct method. Usually can't be earned without the M mark.
B1Independent markA correct fact or value on its own — no method required.
ftFollow-throughMarks for correctly using your own earlier (wrong) answer. One slip doesn't sink the question.
The cheapest marks in GCSE maths: writing one line of method on a question you can't finish. Examiners award M1 for a correct first step constantly — blank space is the only guaranteed zero.
